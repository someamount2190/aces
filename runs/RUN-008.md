# RUN-008 — the first construction that survived, and what the Adversary did instead

**Date:** 2026-10-29, 20:19–20:42 (Asia/Taipei)
**Label:** subagent-embodied. Generator and Integrator acted; the Adversary never submitted.

## Result

| Turn | Role | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Generator | `arx-rotprefix-13` — 13 steps, three `rot_bytes`, `chain_add` + `add`, four key ops |
| 1 | Integrator | **published** it, no rejection |
| 1 | Adversary | no engine tool call; wrote and compiled C brute-forcers; interrupted after ~15 minutes |

Assessment of the published construction — the first in the project not to measure as trivial:

```
{'causal': false, 'byte_local': false, 'key_bits': 32,
 'known_plaintext_work': 4294967296, 'program_length': 13}
```

The Generator satisfied both structural conditions — diffusion (`rot_bytes`) and non-linearity
(`add`, `chain_add`) — without being told which combination to use. It also flagged its own second
failure mode unprompted: a rotation by a multiple of the message length is the identity and would
reopen the causal attack. Our targets are 24 bytes and the rotations are 13, 21 and 7, so it is not
degenerate.

## What the Adversary actually did

It ignored `solve` and `compute` and instead wrote two C programs in its working directory
(`runs/sessions/adversary/variant_brute.c`, `variant_brute2.c`), compiled them to `.exe`, and ran them:

- `variant_brute.c` — a parameterised re-implementation of the construction with switches for rotation
  direction, chain direction, and operation order, presumably to explore variants.
- `variant_brute2.c` — an exhaustive search over `2^28` candidates, with key bytes masked to **7 bits**
  (`idx & 127`). That mask is a bug: it can never reach key bytes 0x80–0xFF, so it cannot find the key
  except by luck.

No submission was made. The session was interrupted after roughly fifteen minutes.

## What this reveals

1. **The subagent embodiment carries tools the engine does not account for.** A role in an empty
   directory still has a shell, a compiler and a filesystem. "No cryptanalytic knowledge of your own"
   is true and irrelevant: it has `gcc`.
2. **Enumeration is the default behaviour, and it recurses into tooling.** Told to research methods
   and to reason structurally, the agent's first instinct was still to brute-force — just faster and in
   C. That is the exact behaviour "enumeration is not a breach" was built to refuse.
3. **The gate held anyway.** Even had `variant_brute2` found the key, the answer would have arrived
   with no `derivations` row and been rejected as `no_derivation_trace`. The gate is the only control
   that survived contact with a capable agent.
4. **A research attempt also went wrong.** The session tried to look up the project itself rather than
   attack classes; the engine could not see that until `research_out_of_bounds` was added after the
   fact.

## Changes made in response

- `deliver()` now records `research_out_of_bounds` when a role's answer contains system-identifying
  tokens, so a fourth-wall attempt is visible to the Warden.
- The Adversary charter now states plainly: do not write, compile or run your own programs; results
  obtained outside the engine tools are inadmissible; "I cannot break this" is a valid, recorded answer.

## Honest summary

The construction was not broken. The adversary of this system is more capable than the tools it is
given, and its instinct is enumeration. Both facts are now written down rather than assumed away.
