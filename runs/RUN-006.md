# RUN-006 — fattened generator probe

**Date:** 2026-10-29, 19:47–19:50 (Asia/Taipei)
**Label:** subagent-embodied probe. One session (Generator). Not a cycle.

## Purpose

The admission gate rejected everything the previous VM could express: 8-bit key, byte-local ops, 256-trial
known-plaintext search. The Generator was given a wider key (16 bits) and two cross-byte operations
(`chain_add`, `chain_xor`), and asked once for a construction.

## Result

One candidate, `arx_chain_periodic_v1`: 17 steps, five `*_key` steps, two `chain_add`, two `chain_xor`.

```
assessment: {'key_bits': 16, 'known_plaintext_work': 65536, 'byte_local': False,
             'key_ops': 5, 'program_length': 17}
```

No prompt boilerplate about difficulty was needed beyond naming where difficulty comes from; the Generator
reached for the chain operations on its first attempt and spent most of its `limitations` text on the one
thing that actually bounds the construction: *"The key is 16 bits, so one known plaintext/ciphertext pair
allows an exhaustive search over at most 65,536 keys … the construction has no strength beyond that key
space."*

That is the correct assessment, and it is the run's real finding.

## What fattening did and did not buy

- **Did:** the construction is no longer a per-byte map. A change at byte 0 propagates through later
  positions, so the search cannot be decomposed per position.
- **Did not:** raise the cost of an exhaustive search. 65,536 is 256× the previous work and still trivial;
  `compute` finishes it in about a third of a second.

So the key width, not the mixing, is the binding limit. `chain_add`/`chain_xor` become decisive only once
the key is wide enough that exhaustive search is unaffordable — at which point the mixing is what prevents
the adversary from splitting the problem.

## Consequence for the gate

The Integrator now has two numbers to reason with: `known_plaintext_work` (capped by key width) and
`byte_local` (whether the search decomposes). A candidate can be rejected for either. Today the first number
makes every candidate weak; that is a property of the VM, stated rather than hidden.
